# Axmed brand film: creative reference library and direction

Nov 15, 2026 · @This Is Wahba

## The brief in one page

We recommend a 60-second film built on one object (a single box of medicine) and the invisible chain of people who move it. It should make the audience feel what Axmed makes possible before they understand what Axmed is.

**The principle:** the technology is the mechanism; the human story is the reason we care. This is not a film about procurement. It is a film about what becomes possible when medicine can reach where it is needed.

**How to use this document:** sections 3–5 are the evidence (real films and the storytelling mechanisms they use). Sections 6–9 are the proposal (five territories, one recommendation, a storyboard and lines). Section 10 is the mood and production direction. Every film listed has a watch link; agency and production credits are given only where a source confirms them.

**What we rejected on purpose:** generic pharma ads, stock footage, doctors smiling at camera, holographic interfaces, robots, glowing blue AI graphics, "AI is transforming healthcare" voiceovers, feature demos, and overly sentimental patient stories.

## The deeper Axmed story

Medicine exists. Access does not reach everyone equally. About 80% of the world's people live in low- and middle-income countries, yet those countries account for less than 6% of global medicine revenue (Axmed fact sheet, pending sign-off).

Between "we need this medicine" and "here it is" sits a chain most people never see:

1. A hospital realises it needs a medicine.
2. A procurement officer has to find it, often from a messy list.
3. A supplier has to be found who can provide it at the right quality.
4. Someone has to verify that it is real, registered and safe.
5. Someone has to negotiate a price the budget can bear.
6. Someone has to move it across borders, roads and the last mile.
7. Someone is waiting for it.

Today that chain is slow, fragmented and expensive. Small buyers are invisible to big suppliers, orders are too small to be worth quoting, and quality is hard to check. Axmed pools demand, lets qualified suppliers compete, checks quality, and moves the order to every site. Sina, Axmed's agent, does the reading and matching that used to take weeks.

**The film's job:** make that invisible chain visible and human for 60 seconds, then let the audience realise someone made it shorter.

## Analysis of each film

Each film is broken down the same way: idea, human story, why we care, structure, the role of technology, visual language, why it works, and the lesson for Axmed. Visual notes come from the films and the credits pages; where a source shares little (Teradata), we say so.

### 1. The Journey Home — Teradata (2025)

- **Idea:** A family's race to be together in a moment of need, quietly carried by data the family never sees.
- **Human story:** A family separated at a critical moment. They want to reach each other in time; the stake is being present when it matters.
- **Why we care:** Time pressure and family. Everyone knows the dread of being far away when something goes wrong.
- **Structure:** Separation → a run of obstacles → each one quietly cleared → arrival. Teradata's page says the film weaves in six real use cases, from fraud prevention to personalised healthcare.
- **Technology:** Invisible. It shows up only as things going right: a flagged card that isn't blocked, a care plan that is ready.
- **Visual language:** Teradata publishes little about the craft; watch for how each "system moment" is shot as an ordinary human beat, not a screen.
- **Why it works:** The audience counts the obstacles, not the features.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Build the film as a journey with checkpoints. Each checkpoint is a place where Axmed removes friction, shown only through what does not go wrong.

### 2. The Walk — Getinge (2018)

- **Idea:** All the teamwork behind an operation comes down to one short walk: the surgeon going to tell the family.
- **Human story:** A surgeon, and a family waiting for news. The stake is a life.
- **Why we care:** The walk is pure suspense: we don't know the outcome until the surgeon arrives.
- **Structure:** The operating team at work → the decision to walk out → the corridor → the family's faces → the news.
- **Technology:** Getinge's equipment is present but never the hero. The walk is.
- **Visual language:** Clinical spaces shot with restraint; the corridor is the set piece, and time slows down.
- **Why it works:** One simple physical action carries the weight of everything that came before.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Find our "walk": one simple physical moment (a box handed over, a fridge door opening) that carries the whole invisible chain behind it.

### 3. Hearing Hands — Samsung (2015)

- **Idea:** An entire neighbourhood learns sign language in secret, so one deaf man can talk with everyone for a day.
- **Human story:** Muharrem, a deaf man in Bagcilar, Istanbul. He wants an ordinary day of being understood.
- **Why we care:** We watch him discover, scene by scene, that the world has quietly changed around him.
- **Structure:** An ordinary morning → small surprises (the baker signs, the taxi driver signs) → rising disbelief → the reveal of who arranged it → tears.
- **Technology:** Almost absent. The service (a video call centre for deaf users) arrives only as the reason behind the day.
- **Visual language:** Hidden cameras, real street, real reactions, handheld and unpolished.
- **Why it works:** The reaction is real, so the emotion is earned.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Let the world arrange itself around the person who needs the medicine, and reveal only at the end that a system made it happen.

### 4. The Greatest — Apple (2022)

- **Idea:** People with disabilities living at full speed, scored to Muhammad Ali's "I am the greatest".
- **Human story:** Many protagonists (a deaf mother alerted when her baby cries, a man editing photos with facial expressions), each wanting independence.
- **Why we care:** Confidence, not pity. The film celebrates; it never asks for sympathy.
- **Structure:** A rhythmic montage of short scenes, building with the music to a crescendo.
- **Technology:** Everywhere, but never explained. Each feature is just the reason someone can do the thing.
- **Visual language:** Bold colour, kinetic editing, music by Spinifex Gum carrying the pace. Director Kim Gehrig.
- **Why it works:** Energy and dignity replace the usual sentimental tone.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Show health workers and procurement teams as capable and proud, not as victims of a broken system.

### 5. Year in Search 2017 — Google (2017)

- **Idea:** The questions the world asked in one year tell the story of that year.
- **Human story:** No single hero; thousands of people searching "how to" in disaster and hope.
- **Why we care:** We recognise our own year in it.
- **Structure:** Hard moments (disasters, fear) → turn to resilience → a rising, hopeful end. Music: Harry Styles' "Sign of the Times".
- **Technology:** Search appears as typed questions; the people are the content.
- **Visual language:** News and real-world footage cut to music, with on-screen queries as the thread.
- **Why it works:** Small individual acts add up to a global portrait.
- **Axmed takeaway:** The order list itself can be our "search bar": real lines ("oxytocin 10 IU", "for 4 district hospitals") as the thread that connects many places.

### 6. Reunion — Google Search (2013)

- **Idea:** A granddaughter uses search to reunite her grandfather with his childhood friend, separated by Partition in 1947.
- **Human story:** Baldev in Delhi, and Yusuf in Lahore. He wants to see his friend again before it's too late; the stake is a lifetime of separation.
- **Why we care:** History and age create urgency; the border creates impossibility.
- **Structure:** An old photo → a small clue (a sweet shop) → a chain of searches and calls across the border → a surprise arrival on Baldev's birthday.
- **Technology:** Explicit but humble: each search is just the next step in the granddaughter's plan.
- **Visual language:** Warm, naturalistic, intimate homes and old city streets. 3:32 long; it plays like a short film.
- **Why it works:** The audience is a step ahead and willing the reunion to happen.
- **Axmed takeaway:** A chain of small, humble steps across borders can be the most emotional thing on screen. Our chain crosses borders too.

### 7. LIVING — Bristol Myers Squibb (\~2021)

- **Idea:** Patients around the world tell their own stories, with no product and no claims.
- **Human story:** Real patients in four countries (including a farmer in rural China) and the people who care for them. They want ordinary life back.
- **Why we care:** It's real, and they're given time.
- **Structure:** Separate lives linked by match cuts into one shared experience.
- **Technology:** None shown; the company's role is implied.
- **Visual language:** Cinéma vérité shot on anamorphic lenses (2.35:1); a touch of magic realism; three continents.
- **Why it works:** Documentary truth with cinematic craft. The match cuts make many stories feel like one.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Use match cuts between continents (a hand on a box in a factory, the same gesture in a clinic) to show one system across many places.

### 8. Wall and Chain — Airbnb (2014)

- **Idea:** A former West German border guard books a Berlin stay and discovers his host was an East German guard.
- **Human story:** A man who left his post in 1987 and never found closure. He wants peace with his past.
- **Why we care:** A real, unresolved wound, healed by an unlikely meeting.
- **Structure:** His past → the trip → the reveal of who the host is → reconciliation.
- **Technology:** Booking is a single beat; belonging is the product.
- **Visual language:** Animation (Psyop) lets a true story feel like a fable; around 75 seconds.
- **Why it works:** It sells the outcome (belonging), never the platform.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Sell "medicine arrives" and "people are reached", not "procurement software". Animation or stylisation is a valid route if live action is too costly.

### 9. Giving — TrueMove H (2013)

- **Idea:** A small act of kindness over stolen medicine comes back 30 years later as a life saved.
- **Human story:** A boy caught stealing medicine for his sick mother; a noodle vendor pays for it. Thirty years later the vendor is in hospital, and the bill is huge.
- **Why we care:** Medicine as the thing you'd steal for. Then the debt of kindness repaid.
- **Structure:** The theft → the vendor's gift → a 30-year time jump → the vendor collapses → the bill paid by his doctor, the boy grown up.
- **Technology:** None. The telecom brand appears only at the end with "Giving is the best communication".
- **Visual language:** Naturalistic Bangkok streets, warm melodrama, a 3-minute short film with a slow reveal.
- **Why it works:** The reveal reframes everything; we rewatch the opening in our heads.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Medicine already carries life-or-death weight. Our reveal can reframe the opening too: the box we saw at the start was the one that saved her.

### 10. Unsung Hero — Thai Life Insurance (2014)

- **Idea:** A man does small, unnoticed good deeds every day; what he gets back is invisible but everything.
- **Human story:** An ordinary man who waters a plant, feeds a dog and helps a begging girl into school. He wants nothing back.
- **Why we care:** Repetition builds, then the payoff shows us the compound effect of tiny acts.
- **Structure:** Daily loop → people mock him → the loop continues → one day the results bloom all at once.
- **Technology:** None.
- **Visual language:** Simple, repeated street compositions so we notice change; music swells at the payoff. Dir. Thanonchai Sornsriwichai.
- **Why it works:** The repeated loop lets the audience feel time and accumulation without explanation.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Repetition is a tool: the same clinic shelf, empty week after week, then one day it's stocked. We feel the change without a word of copy.

### 11. Help a Child Reach 5 — Lifebuoy (2013)

- **Idea:** A father walks on his hands to a hilltop temple because his son made it to age five.
- **Human story:** A father in Thesgora, India; his son Muthu is the only one of his children to survive to five.
- **Why we care:** The ritual is strange, then the reason lands: children dying of preventable disease.
- **Structure:** A boy follows handprints → a procession → the climb → the father explains → the statistic and the call to action.
- **Technology:** None. The product (handwashing) appears only as the solution in the end card.
- **Visual language:** Real village, real people, music and procession; documentary feel.
- **Why it works:** A joyful ritual hides a devastating truth, revealed late.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Lead with life and joy, and reveal the stake late. A celebration that only happens because a medicine arrived.

### 12. Javier in Frame — Google Pixel (2024)

- **Idea:** A blind man photographs his own family's milestones using a phone that guides his frame.
- **Human story:** Javier and his partner Nikki start a family. He wants to capture their life himself.
- **Why we care:** Authorship. The pictures are his, not someone else's.
- **Structure:** Meeting → milestones (engagement, wedding, baby) as photos he takes → the final image.
- **Technology:** Explicit but humble: an AI feature that speaks guidance; the human still presses the shutter.
- **Visual language:** Shot from a low-vision point of view at times (blur, light), directed by Adam Morse, who is blind. 60 seconds.
- **Why it works:** AI is a quiet partner; the human keeps the decision and the credit.
- **Axmed takeaway:** This is our model for Sina: the procurement officer stays the decision-maker; the AI reads, checks and suggests.

### 13. The Delivery — CEPI, Gavi, UNICEF, WHO (2023)

- **Idea:** The story of vaccine delivery told through one cold box carried across remote landscapes.
- **Human story:** The people who carry vaccines on the last mile.
- **Why we care:** The object is precious and the terrain is hard.
- **Structure:** A cold box passed through a run of landscapes, one carrier at a time, opening an online experience with data and archive images.
- **Technology:** The cold chain is shown physically, never as an interface.
- **Visual language:** A digital replica of the real cold box moving through landscapes (FutureDeluxe); shown in the WHO headquarters foyer in Geneva.
- **Why it works:** One object makes a vast system easy to follow.
- **Axmed takeaway:** The clearest proof that "follow the object" works for medicine. Our box should be specific and recognisable, as theirs is.

### 14. The Heart of Trade — Maersk (\~2017)

- **Idea:** Trade is shown through the people and communities it lifts, not the ships.
- **Human story:** Entrepreneurs in China, Africa and the USA whose businesses depend on goods arriving.
- **Why we care:** Infrastructure becomes livelihoods we can see.
- **Structure:** A main film plus short case films, each a person and the community around them.
- **Technology:** Ships and containers are context; the people are the subject.
- **Visual language:** Director Malcolm Green travelled worldwide; documentary portraits.
- **Why it works:** It humanises a company that moves boxes, the same challenge Axmed has.
- **Axmed takeaway:** Pair the main film with short real stories (a district pharmacist, a supplier, a procurement officer) to make the system human.

### 15. Dear Sophie — Google Chrome (2011)

- **Idea:** A father writes emails to his newborn daughter, to read when she grows up.
- **Human story:** A dad documenting Sophie's life, from birth onwards.
- **Why we care:** Love told through small digital acts.
- **Structure:** A new account created → a run of emails, photos and videos over years → the final message.
- **Technology:** The browser screen is the whole film, yet it reads purely as a love letter.
- **Visual language:** Screen recording only, with typing, attachments and a piano score.
- **Why it works:** It proves a screen can be emotional when what is typed matters.
- **Axmed takeaway:** If we ever show Sina's screen, the lines must carry the emotion ("Misoprostol for 4 district hospitals"), never the features.

### 16. We All Win — Microsoft Xbox (2019)

- **Idea:** Kids with limb differences play alongside friends, thanks to a controller designed for them.
- **Human story:** Real children who want to play like everyone else.
- **Why we care:** Belonging; their own voices tell it.
- **Structure:** The kids explain what they couldn't do → the controller → playing and winning together.
- **Technology:** Shown clearly, but always in a child's hands.
- **Visual language:** Documentary interviews at home, bright and natural. A 60-second Super Bowl spot.
- **Why it works:** Real people speaking for themselves; the product only removes a barrier.
- **Axmed takeaway:** If we use real procurement officers or pharmacists, let them speak in their own words about what used to stop them.

## Patterns across the films

The strongest films use one mechanism cleanly, not five at once. For Axmed, three mechanisms do the most work: following the medicine (A), revealing the invisible system (B and G), and keeping the human as the decision-maker (H).

| Mechanism | What it does | Best examples | Fit for Axmed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. The medicine journey | Follow one object through many hands until it reaches the person who needs it | The Delivery, Giving | Highest: it is literally what Axmed does |
| B. Invisible infrastructure | Show the huge system behind something simple | The Journey Home, The Walk, The Heart of Trade | High |
| C. One story → global system | Start with one person, widen to a network | Year in Search, LIVING | High, for the ending |
| D. Before / after | Frustration, then transformation | Unsung Hero, Help a Child Reach 5 | Medium: risks becoming a demo |
| E. The countdown | Will it arrive in time? | The Journey Home, Reunion | High: gives a 60s film its tension |
| F. The chain reaction | One small action sets off events across places | Giving, Reunion | High: the order list is the first domino |
| G. The unexpected reveal | We think it's about X, it's about the system behind X | Hearing Hands, Giving, Help a Child Reach 5 | Highest for TV: the reveal is where Axmed lands |
| H. Human + AI partnership | AI helps; the human decides | Javier in Frame, Dear Sophie | High: this is Sina's role |
| I. Documentary realism | Feels witnessed, not staged | Hearing Hands, LIVING, We All Win | High for trust in health |

**Seven principles we take from them**

1. **One object, one through-line.** The audience can follow a cold box or a sweet shop across a border; it cannot follow "procurement".
2. **Hide the brand until the reveal.** Samsung, TrueMove and Lifebuoy all land the brand in the last seconds, after we already care.
3. **The product is a reason, not a scene.** Technology explains why the world changed; it never takes the frame.
4. **Dignity over pity.** Apple and Microsoft show capable people. Health workers in low-income countries should look skilled and proud, never helpless.
5. **Time is the tension.** A deadline (a birthday, a birth, a stock-out) turns logistics into suspense.
6. **Real textures beat polish.** Hidden cameras, vérité, real places. Anamorphic craft is welcome; staged perfection is not.
7. **One small physical act carries everything.** The surgeon's walk, the father's handstand. Find Axmed's single gesture.

## Five creative territories

Five genuinely different ways in: an order line, a box, a rewind, a shelf, and two strangers. Only one of them has a doctor in it, and nobody smiles at the camera.

### 1. The List

- **One-line idea:** Every line on an order list is a person waiting.
- **Protagonist:** Grace, a procurement officer at a regional health office, working late.
- **Opening shot:** Extreme close-up of a cursor blinking on a messy spreadsheet line: "Oxy 10IU amp x 4000 ??".
- **Story:** As Grace works the list, each line dissolves into the people behind it: 4,000 ampoules become births in four district hospitals. A supplier in another country reads the same line. A pharmacist checks a delivery against it.
- **Turning point:** The line Grace almost couldn't fill (the "??") becomes a midwife's hand reaching into a fridge, finding the ampoule there.
- **How Axmed enters:** Quietly, as Sina tidying the list beside Grace: matching, asking one question. Grace makes the call.
- **Final shot:** Grace closes the laptop. Cut to a newborn's first cry in a district hospital, off-screen, as a nurse writes the time on a chart.
- **Tagline:** "Every line is someone."
- **Why it could work:** It turns Axmed's literal front door (the order list) into the emotional device. Nobody else can own it.

### 2. The Last Box

- **One-line idea:** One box of heat-stable medicine, followed from factory to the moment it's needed.
- **Protagonist:** The box, passed hand to hand; the final human is a midwife on a night shift.
- **Opening shot:** A single carton coming off a line in a quiet factory at dawn, a worker's hand smoothing the label.
- **Story:** The box moves through a port, a warehouse, a truck on red-earth roads, a motorbike, a clinic storeroom. A clock is never shown, but light changes from dawn to night.
- **Turning point:** At the clinic, the power flickers. The midwife reaches for the box: it's there, and it doesn't need a fridge.
- **How Axmed enters:** A single route line drawn across the journey at the end, joining every hand that touched it.
- **Final shot:** The empty box on a clinic shelf at sunrise, next to the window.
- **Tagline:** "Every medicine has a journey. We make it shorter."
- **Why it could work:** The clearest story, proven by The Delivery. Risk: it is the most expected idea.

### 3. Everyone Who Touched It

- **One-line idea:** Rewind a child's recovery through every person who made it possible.
- **Protagonist:** A girl, eight, running back into her classroom after being ill.
- **Opening shot:** The girl bursting through a classroom door, friends turning.
- **Story:** The film rewinds: the clinic visit, the nurse, the delivery driver, the warehouse, the quality checker, the supplier, the negotiation, back to the first email. Each person is visible for one second.
- **Turning point:** The rewind slows at a long, frozen gap: weeks of nothing, a stamp waiting. Then it skips forward, and the gap collapses.
- **How Axmed enters:** As the reason the gap collapses: "Weeks became days."
- **Final shot:** Forward again at normal speed: the girl in class, raising her hand.
- **Tagline:** "It takes a world to reach one child. We help it move faster."
- **Why it could work:** A striking, ownable device and very TV-friendly. Rewind sequences also suit AI video well (short shots, one action each).

### 4. The Shelf

- **One-line idea:** The same shelf, week after week, until it isn't empty.
- **Protagonist:** Amina, a pharmacist in a district health centre.
- **Opening shot:** A locked-off frame of a half-empty medicine shelf, Monday 07:00.
- **Story:** The same frame, week after week. Gaps. A mother turned away. Amina writing "out of stock" again. The seasons change through the window.
- **Turning point:** One Monday the frame is the same, but the shelf is full, and Amina hands a box across the counter.
- **How Axmed enters:** Not shown. A single end line explains the change.
- **Final shot:** The full shelf; Amina's handwritten "out of stock" note lies crumpled in the bin.
- **Tagline:** "Out of stock shouldn't be a diagnosis."
- **Why it could work:** Minimal, cheap to make, powerful repetition (as in Unsung Hero). Risk: Axmed can feel bolted on.

### 5. Two Mornings

- **One-line idea:** A factory worker and a nurse who will never meet, connected by what one makes and the other gives.
- **Protagonists:** Ravi, a line supervisor in a pharma plant in India; Esther, a nurse in Malawi.
- **Opening shot:** Split screen: two alarm clocks, two cups of tea, two commutes, at the same time.
- **Story:** Their days mirror each other through match cuts (hands washing, gloves on, a careful check). Ravi releases a batch; Esther's clinic gets a delivery.
- **Turning point:** The split screen closes into one frame: Esther holds the box Ravi signed off, his initials on the batch label.
- **How Axmed enters:** As the line between their two halves of the screen, the connection that used to be missing.
- **Final shot:** Esther's patient. Ravi, at home, looking at his daughter.
- **Tagline:** "Made somewhere. Needed everywhere. Connected by Axmed."
- **Why it could work:** It dignifies both sides of the marketplace (suppliers and buyers), not just patients.

## Recommendation: The List

We recommend **The List**: every line on an order list is a person waiting. It scores highest overall (41 of 45) and is the only territory no other company could run, because the order list is Axmed's own front door.

**Why The List**

- **It makes the invisible visible in Axmed's own terms.** A messy line like "Oxy 10IU amp x 4000 ??" is exactly what Sina reads. Turning it into 4,000 births is the whole company in one image.
- **It honours the unsung hero.** The procurement officer is rarely seen and never celebrated. Making her the protagonist is fresh and true.
- **Human and AI, in the right order.** Sina reads, matches and asks one question; Grace decides. Just like Javier in Frame, the person keeps the credit.
- **The reveal lands where the brand lands.** We think we're watching paperwork; we're watching births. The brand arrives as the reason.
- **It suits modern AI video.** Short shots, one action each, many places, almost no dialogue. The spreadsheet and the UI are designed, not filmed.

**What we borrow from the runner-up:** the collapsing gap from Everyone Who Touched It (weeks of waiting shrinking to days) becomes one beat inside The List.

## Storyboard: The List, 60 seconds

Fourteen shots in three acts: the line (0–18s), the people behind it (18–45s), the arrival and the reveal (45–60s). No voiceover until the last 8 seconds; sound and the typed list carry the story.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Notes for the director**

- **The "??" line is the spine.** It appears in shots 1, 3–4 and 9; the audience should feel it resolve.
- **Sina stays small.** A quiet panel beside Grace's work, never a hologram, never a face. Grace makes every decision.
- **One second per person** in shots 5–9 (as in the rewind territory) keeps the chain fast and the film inside 60 seconds.
- **Cutdowns:** a 30s keeps shots 1, 3, 4, 7, 10, 11, 14; a 15s keeps 1, 10, 11, 14.
- **Illustrative UI and data.** The 4,000 ampoules and four hospitals are story numbers, not claims; label them in any version that could be read as a case study.

## Taglines and final lines

Our lead line is **"Every line is someone."** It belongs only to The List, it's five words, and it works in any language. It sits above the existing brand line, "Accelerating access to healthcare for all."

| Line | Best with | Why | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every line is someone. | The List | Turns the order list into people; ownable | Needs the film to land; weak on its own in print |
| Behind every order, someone is waiting. | The List, The Shelf | Clear and human | Slightly explanatory |
| From "we need it" to "here it is". | The Last Box, The List | Names the gap Axmed closes | Quotation marks are hard in some languages |
| Every medicine has a journey. We make it shorter. | The Last Box | Your starting territory; clear benefit | Familiar; risks sounding like logistics |
| It takes a world to reach one child. | Everyone Who Touched It | Big, emotional, global | Child-centred lines can feel sentimental |
| Weeks became days. | Everyone Who Touched It, The List | A concrete promise | Needs proof before it airs |
| Out of stock shouldn't be a diagnosis. | The Shelf | Sharp, memorable | Could read as blaming health workers |
| Made somewhere. Needed everywhere. | Two Mornings | Speaks to suppliers and buyers | Less emotional |
| Medicine exists. Now it can arrive. | Any | States the human truth simply | "Now" implies a before; check the tone |
| So the medicine is there when the moment comes. | The List, The Last Box | Captures the midwife beat | Long for an end card |

**Rule for any final line:** no "AI", no "platform", no "procurement". The film shows the mechanism; the line names the human outcome. "Weeks became days" needs sign-off against real delivery data before it runs.

## Visual reference board and mood

The look is documentary realism with cinematic restraint: LIVING's anamorphic vérité, the intimacy of Reunion, and Hearing Hands' real streets. Warm human light in the world, cool indigo at night, and one cyan accent for the moment medicine arrives.

| Element | Direction | Reference to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera | Handheld but calm, close on hands and faces; locked-off frames for the office and the storeroom | LIVING, Hearing Hands |
| Lens and format | Anamorphic 2.39:1, shallow depth of field, real grain | LIVING |
| Light | Practical sources only: desk lamps, fridge light, dawn windows, headlights. No glossy studio light | Reunion, The Walk |
| Colour | Warm skin and earth (red roads, ochre walls) against deep indigo nights; cyan used once, on the ampoule or the fridge light | Axmed brand palette |
| Locations | Regional health office, a pharma plant QA lab, a port warehouse, red-earth roads, a district hospital storeroom, a labour ward | The Heart of Trade, The Delivery |
| People | Real-looking African and South Asian health workers and suppliers, capable and proud; no pity, no posing | The Greatest, We All Win |
| Edit | Match cuts on gestures (click to click, hand to hand); one second per person in the chain; one long held beat in the labour ward | LIVING, Year in Search |
| Sound | Real room tone carries the first half; a low string score enters on the first match cut; silence before the first cry | The Walk, Help a Child Reach 5 |
| Screens | Designed, not filmed: the order list and Sina's panel in the Axmed UI style, calm and small | Dear Sophie, Javier in Frame |
| Type | Instrument Serif for the end line on an indigo field, as on the website | Axmed brand system |

**Never on screen:** holograms, glowing blue AI graphics, robots, a doctor smiling at camera, stock hospital corridors, pills tumbling in slow motion, or a face for Sina.

**Producing with AI video tools**

- **Shot length:** keep each generated shot to 3–5 seconds with one action. The storyboard is written that way.
- **Consistency:** lock Grace and the midwife as reusable character references. Generate stills first (Higgsfield or similar), then animate.
- **The UI:** build the spreadsheet and Sina's panel as real motion graphics from the Axmed design system, not generated text, so every word is legible and correct.
- **Hands and labels:** generate the ampoule, the fridge and the box labels as clean plates, and comp the real text in afterwards.
- **Honesty:** if people are AI-generated, don't present them as real Axmed partners. Add a small "dramatisation" note in digital versions.
- **Assets already in the repo:** mood photos in `brand/photos/generated/`, your reference screenshots in `brand/moodboard/refs/`, and the colour and type tokens in `brand/system.css`.
